Can plume monitoring flag a large U-Net forecast error one snapshot ahead?

The previous change in the full simulated footprint flagged 23 of 24 large contractions on 733 paired evaluation cases. The same alarm flagged only 10 of 45 high-error U-Net forecasts. Plume change can warrant a closer look, but this alarm missed most of the largest forecast errors.

Where did we sample the plume?

A site is one cell in a 128 × 100 groundwater simulation. Imagine being able to read concentration at only 16 cells. The original layout spreads them across the grid. The central layout places all 16 in a smaller rectangle around the grid center. Each layout stays fixed across cases and time. For each one, we measure the change at every site between snapshots 1 and 2 and combine the 16 changes into one root-mean-square score. Each layout raises an alarm above its own reference threshold. The full-field benchmark uses the entire simulated plume map.

Moving the 16 sites inward detected 105 of 142 large contractions, up from zero with the original layout. False alarms rose from 229 to 261 among 4,858 other trajectories. The perimeter layout never fired. We chose the two alternatives after seeing the plume maps and tested them on the same trajectories. This makes the central layout a candidate for testing on new cases.

Only 6 of the 24 contractions also had high forecast error. High errors were more frequent among full-field alerts (10/55, 18.2%) than among cases without an alert (35/678, 5.2%). The rule missed 35 of the 45 high-error forecasts. At the original 16 sites, just 1 of 39 alerts corresponded to high error.

Which forecasts were evaluated?

We recovered 1,491 original U-Net test predictions for snapshot 3 and checked their ordering against fresh checkpoint inference on 16 rows. Error is the spatial RMSE against eSTOMP over the 128 × 100 physical grid, excluding padding. The 95th percentile across 758 paired reference cases set the high-error cutoff at 1.164 µg/L. The other 733 paired cases supplied the results above. The original U-Net split by snapshot, and 716 of these trajectories had another snapshot in training. This limits inference about new trajectories. The model is from the earlier U-Net study. Yilin’s replay results below come from a separate comparison.

Why a large sensor change missed the events

All 142 events were footprint contractions. The median concentration-change score at the 16 locations was 15.1 for event trajectories and 27.5 for the others, in the archive’s concentration units. An alarm that fires on a large score therefore looks in the wrong direction in this test. The previous full-field footprint change tracked the coming contraction more closely. Even that signal produced 230 false alarms among its 370 alerts.

How we tested it

We reused the archived eSTOMP concentration fields from dataset 6. Each of 10,000 trajectories contributed one decision at snapshot 2. The sparse score is the root-mean-square concentration change from snapshots 1 to 2 on a fixed 4 × 4 grid. These are ideal, noise-free simulated observations. The benchmark uses the relative change in the number of grid cells containing 90% of plume mass over the same interval.

We assigned 5,000 trajectories to threshold selection and 5,000 to evaluation by seeded case ID shuffle. A large change was an absolute relative footprint change of at least 34.30% from snapshot 2 to 3, the reference-set 97th percentile. Each rule fired above its own 95th percentile among reference non-events. The event rate in evaluation was 142 / 5,000 (2.84%). The footprint definition follows the adaptive-replay study. This threshold and these trajectories belong to the new pilot. The comparison changes both spatial coverage and the measured quantity, so it cannot isolate the effect of sensor count.

What the existing U-Net update achieved

In Yilin’s separate offline replay study, fixed replay reduced rare-regime RMSE from 10.04 to 1.21 µg/L. Historical RMSE rose from 0.58 to 1.03 µg/L. Those results establish why an update decision needs evidence of both new-regime error and historical retention. The replay outcomes have yet to be paired with the alerts above.
